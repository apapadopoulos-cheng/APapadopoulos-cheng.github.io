About

I am a PhD candidate in Chemical Engineering at the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, working in the Laboratory of Inorganic Chemistry & Advanced Materials.

My research is driven by a simple question: how can we use inorganic chemistry to design molecules with new and controllable properties?

My work focuses on the synthesis and characterization of metal–organic vanadium compounds, with particular emphasis on understanding how changes in oxidation state, ligand coordination, and molecular structure influence their chemical and biological behavior.

I enjoy working at the intersection of different areas of chemistry, from molecular synthesis and coordination chemistry to spectroscopy, electrochemistry, and biological evaluation. For me, research is not only about preparing new compounds, but also about understanding why they behave the way they do.

Research

Vanadium Chemistry

My primary research interest is the design, synthesis, and evaluation of vanadium coordination compounds across different oxidation states.

I investigate how the nature of the organic ligand, the coordination environment, the oxidation state, and the reaction conditions can be systematically controlled to access new vanadium architectures.

Particular emphasis is placed on V(III), V(IV), and V(V) chemistry, including vanadium complexes with biologically relevant α-hydroxycarboxylic acids.

From Structure to Function

The synthesis of a new compound is only the beginning. I combine structural and physicochemical characterization with studies of stability, redox behavior, aqueous speciation, and biological activity to establish relationships between molecular structure and function.

My broader goal is to understand how controlled coordination chemistry can be used to create vanadium compounds with specific and potentially useful properties.

My Research Journey

My interest in inorganic chemistry has grown through working with different metal–organic systems and exploring how subtle changes at the molecular level can lead to very different chemical properties.

Alongside my vanadium research, I have worked on the synthesis and characterization of lanthanide–flavonoid compounds, an experience that strengthened my interest in coordination chemistry and in the relationship between metal ions and organic ligands.

Today, my research is centered on vanadium, an element with an unusually rich redox chemistry and a remarkable diversity of coordination environments. This makes it both challenging and exciting to study.

I am particularly interested in chemistry that goes beyond simply preparing a compound: controlling its formation, understanding its structure and reactivity, and ultimately connecting its molecular properties with biological function.

Looking Ahead

My research interests continue to expand toward the connection between coordination chemistry, nanomaterials, and biological systems. I am particularly interested in developing strategies that combine the molecular precision of coordination chemistry with the functional properties of nanoscale platforms, creating new approaches for studying biologically relevant molecular targets.

I hope to continue developing as a researcher through international collaborations, interdisciplinary projects, and research that connects fundamental inorganic chemistry with meaningful biological questions.
